My research develops numerical algorithms and machine-learning models for partial-differential-equation-based physics, and implements them in parallel software that runs at scale. The application areas are fusion and high-energy-density plasmas, atmospheric flows, compressible aerodynamics, and epidemiology.

Scientific machine learning

Simulation data and solvers for machine-learning studies

Most of my involvement in machine learning has been from the physics side. I supply the solvers and generate the training data that the learned models are built on, and co-author the resulting papers.

  • HyPar provided the electrostatic plasma simulations behind local reduced-order models built by physics-informed decomposition of the solution manifold (Comput. Phys. Commun., 2026), and the earlier latent-space dynamics identification work (GPLaSDI, Comput. Methods Appl. Mech. Eng., 2024).
  • The super-droplet microphysics capability I developed in ERF generated the training data for data-driven reduced-order modeling of warm-rain microphysics, led by Emily de Jong (JGR: Machine Learning and Computation, 2026).

Learned denoisers for particle-in-cell data

This is early-stage exploratory work, begun in 2026. It is not published and the results so far are preliminary.

Particle-in-cell methods represent a plasma by a finite sample of macroparticles. Grid quantities deposited from that sample carry Monte-Carlo noise that decays only as the inverse square root of the particle count. Accuracy therefore costs particles, and particles dominate memory and compute. We are examining whether a neural denoiser trained on paired low- and high-particle-count snapshots can reduce that cost. The work compares conditioned U-Net, diffusion, and local convolutional architectures, with physics-informed terms in the training objective.

Numerical methods

  • High-resolution finite-difference and finite-volume methods for hyperbolic conservation laws.
  • Nonlinear, non-oscillatory compact schemes. The CRWENO family couples compact reconstruction with weighted essentially non-oscillatory limiting, giving higher spectral resolution at a given formal order.
  • Multi-stage implicit and implicit-explicit (IMEX) time integration for multiscale systems, including characteristic-based flux partitioning for low-Mach-number and gravity-stratified flows.
  • Multirate and extrapolated multirate methods for adaptive-mesh-refinement solvers.
  • Well-balanced discretizations that preserve hydrostatic balance to machine precision.
  • Preconditioning and nonlinear solver design for stiff multiphysics systems.

Particle-based methods

  • Super-droplet cloud microphysics, including ice processes, coupled to a performance-portable atmospheric solver.
  • Fully implicit, exactly energy- and charge-conserving particle-in-cell algorithms for dense plasmas, with Monte-Carlo Coulomb collisions.
  • Agent-based epidemiological models, where each agent carries an individual disease and behaviour state.

Plasma simulation

  • Continuum gyrokinetic and drift-kinetic modeling of the tokamak edge, on mapped multi-block grids with high-order finite-volume discretization.
  • Semi-implicit time integration of the Vlasov-Fokker-Planck system, treating the stiff collisional and parallel-streaming terms implicitly.
  • Multifluid models for interpenetrating, counterstreaming plasmas in inertial confinement fusion.
  • Nonlinear hydrodynamics with photochemistry, for plasma-based and gaseous optical elements.

High-performance computing

  • Scalable implementation of nonlinear compact schemes, which require solving distributed tridiagonal systems at every reconstruction. Our iterative substructuring approach removed the serial bottleneck and demonstrated scaling on DOE leadership-class platforms.
  • GPU-accelerated direct numerical simulation of compressible turbulent flows.
  • Performance-portable implementations over AMReX, and distributed multi-GPU training for the machine-learning work.

Earlier research

  • Derivation, analysis, and parallel implementation of the CRWENO schemes (Ph.D., University of Maryland; adviser James Baeder).
  • Time integrators in PETSc: multi-stage implicit, explicit and IMEX Runge-Kutta methods, and general linear methods with global error estimation.
  • Semi-implicit and multirate time integration in NUMA, a non-hydrostatic atmospheric model.
  • A three-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes solver with immersed boundaries, applied to vortex-ring and wall interactions and to rotorcraft wake modeling.
  • Probability density function methods for power systems with uncertain input.
  • High-order ENO and WENO schemes for ideal magnetohydrodynamics, and the effect of non-convexity on scheme convergence (M.Tech., IIT Bombay; adviser Avijit Chatterjee).
  • Finite-volume time-domain solution of Maxwell's equations, used to predict the radar cross-section of low-observable aircraft.
